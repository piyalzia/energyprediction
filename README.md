# Predicting organic molecule energies based on DFT and deploying an artificial neural network 

In quantum chemistry, identifying the energy of organic molecules is key to understanding core biological and chemical processes. However, the existing methods to model these energies present various challenges: semi-empirical methods have low accuracy due to approximations set to reduce computational load and empirical interatomic potentials specialize in near equilibrium states. Behler and Parinello allowed for neural networks to be used to predict DFT energy surfaces by formulating symmetry functions. Smith et al. expanded on neural network utilization by exploring the capability of neural networks on more complex organic structures and larger datasets.

In my work, to compare performance differences from Smith et al. and aim for increased accuracy outcomes, I explore architectural modifications and various hyperparameter tuning configurations.
